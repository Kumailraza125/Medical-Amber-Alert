4.​ How do you know it’s real? Name at least one person you spoke to who has this
    problem.
    William Yeung (Paramedic in the province of BC Hospitals) focuses on the pre-hospital
    care from 911 dispatch and patient transport to hospital triage handoffs, highlighting the
    critical goal of transferring care, so paramedics can return to community emergencies.
         -​ Operational invisibility: Paramedics and computer-aided dispatch (CAD) lack
             visibility into hospital bed availability, trauma team, or real time capacity until the
             physical care arrival.
         -​ Shift overruns: Rural ER closures extend standard 12 hour shifts by 3 to 6
             hours due to forced long distance transfers, pushing paramedics to the 16 hour
             mandatory driving limit.
         -​ Offload bottleneck: Triage delays take 1-2 hours before the incoming patient
             assessment begins, which paramedics spend significantly longer time of shift
             holding patients in hallways.
         -​ Information friction during transport: Active driving and hands on patient care
             prevent ambulance paramedics from reading computer-aided dispatch (CAD) text
             alerts on the road, creating an information lag during critical transport decisions.
         -​ Isolated systems: BC regional hospitals operate on separate IT networks. The
             Patient Transfer Network (PTN) only manages interfacility transfers (between two
             healthcares), leaving emergency dispatch blind to real-time ER status.
